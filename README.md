HR Attrition Dashboard

Business Problem
For this project, I wanted to use Power BI to understand employee attrition and see if there were any clear patterns in the data. 
I mainly wanted to find out which departments and job roles had more employees leaving, and whether things like gender and 
overtime made a noticeable difference.

Dataset
IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition Dataset (Kaggle)

The dataset has information about 1,470 employees. It includes things like age, gender, department, job role, overtime, salary, 
job satisfaction and other employee related information.

Tools
Power BI, DAX, Power Query, Cards, Bar and Column Charts, Slicers

Dashboard

Key Insights
1. Overall Attrition: The overall attrition rate was 16.12%, with 237 out of 1,470 employees leaving.
   Sales had the highest attrition at 20.63%, while R&D had the lowest at 13.84%.
3. Gender Difference: The results changed when I looked at gender. For males, Sales had the highest attrition, followed by R&D and HR.
   For females, HR had the highest, followed by Sales and R&D. This difference wasn't visible when looking at departments alone.
4. Job Role: Sales Representatives had the highest attrition rate, at close to 40%. This was much higher than the overall company rate of 16.12%.
5. Overtime: Employees who worked overtime had an attrition rate of around 30 to 34%, compared with about 10% for those who didn't.
   This was one of the biggest differences I found in the data.
   
Other Factors: I mainly looked at department, job role, gender and overtime in this dashboard. 
Salary, years at the company and job satisfaction could be looked at in more detail in a future analysis.

What I Would Look At Next
The overtime difference would be the first thing I'd want to investigate further.
It would be interesting to see if the same pattern appears across different departments and job roles. 
I'd also look more closely at the female employees in HR because their attrition rate was noticeably higher in the gender breakdown. 
After that, I'd look at salary, years at the company and job satisfaction to see if they give any more explanation for the patterns I found.
